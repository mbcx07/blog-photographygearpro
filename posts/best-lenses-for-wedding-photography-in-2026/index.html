Best Lenses for Wedding Photography in 2026

2026-07-18 · Lenses · Tags: wedding photography, lenses, portrait lens, event photography
## The Essential Wedding Photography Lens Kit Wedding photography demands versatility, speed, and reliability. You need to capture everything from wide establishing shots to intimate portraits, often in challenging light. Here's our recommended lens kit for every budget. ### The Professional Kit ($6,000+) **Sony 24-70mm f/2.8 GM II ($2,300)** The workhorse. This lens lives on one camera body for 80% of the day. The GM II is sharper, lighter, and focuses faster than its predecessor. At 24mm, it captures the ceremony venue and reception hall. At 70mm, it's perfect for couple portraits and detail shots. **Sony 70-200mm f/2.8 GM II ($2,800)** The ceremony lens. From the back of the church, this lens captures intimate moments without being intrusive. The compression at 200mm creates beautiful portraits with creamy backgrounds. The OSS stabilization is excellent for handheld shooting in dim reception lighting. **Sony 35mm f/1.4 GM ($1,400)** The storytelling lens. Wide enough for environmental portraits, fast enough for low-light reception dancing. The f/1.4 aperture creates dreamy images with that unmistakable prime-lens look. **Sony 85mm f/1.4 GM ($1,800)** The portrait specialist. For formal portraits, detail shots, and those magical backlit shots during golden hour. The 85mm perspective is universally flattering for portraits. ### The Enthusiast Kit ($3,000) **Tamron 35-150mm f/2-2.8 ($1,900)** The ultimate wedding zoom. This lens covers the most useful range for weddings with a fast f/2 aperture at the wide end. It replaces both a 24-70mm and 70-200mm for many photographers. The only compromise is the lack of 24mm for wide shots. **Sigma 24mm f/1.4 Art ($800)** For wide reception shots, dance floor action, and dramatic ceremony angles. The f/1.4 aperture is invaluable in dark reception venues. **Sony 50mm f/1.2 GM ($1,600)** The low-light hero. f/1.2 gathers 4x more light than f/2.8, making it possible to shoot in candlelit ceremonies without flash. The rendering is gorgeous. ### The Budget Kit ($1,500) **Tamron 28-75mm f/2.8 G2 ($900)** The budget workhorse. 90% of the quality of the Sony GM at half the price. Sharp, fast-focusing, and compact. **Sony 85mm f/1.8 ($600)** The best value lens in Sony's lineup. Sharp wide open, fast autofocus, and beautiful rendering. For portraits, it's 90% of the GM at one-third the price. **Samyang 35mm f/1.8 ($400)** Compact, sharp, and affordable. Perfect for a second body or as a walk-around lens during reception. ### Our Strategy **Two-body setup:** The most reliable wedding kit is two camera bodies. Body A has the 24-70mm (or 35-150mm). Body B has the 70-200mm (or 85mm). This eliminates lens changes during critical moments. **Backup everything:** Bring a third body and duplicate your most important focal lengths. A failed lens during the first dance is a disaster. **Prime vs zoom:** Use zooms for the ceremony and reception (speed matters). Use primes for portraits and detail shots (quality matters). **The one-lens challenge:** If you could only bring one lens to a wedding, make it the Tamron 35-150mm f/2-2.8. It covers 90% of wedding photography needs in a single lens.
