How to Clean Your Camera Sensor

Every photographer eventually faces the dreaded dust spot. Tripod en Amazon. Those dark circles in the sky, visible at f/16 and impossible to clone out of 500 photos. Here's how to clean your sensor safely.

When to Clean Your Sensor

Signs you need a cleaning:
- Dark spots visible in the same position across multiple images
- Spots are most visible at small apertures (f/11-f/22)
- Spots appear in areas of solid color (sky, walls)
- Your camera's sensor cleaning function isn't helping

How to check:
1. Set your lens to f/16 or f/22
2. Photograph a plain white wall or bright sky
3. Review the image at 100% on your computer
4. If you see spots, it's time to clean

What NOT to Do

Never use:
- Compressed air (can spray propellant onto sensor)
- Canned air (too cold, can cause condensation)
- Tissues or paper towels (scratch the sensor)
- Your breath (moisture and saliva)
- Any liquid not designed for sensor cleaning

Method 1: The Air Blower (For Light Dust)

Best for: A few dust specks, no stubborn spots

What you need:
- A rocket air blower (Giotto's Rocket or similar, $10-15)
- A clean, dust-free environment (bathroom after a hot shower works well)

Steps:
1. Power off your camera
2. Remove the lens
3. Lock the mirror up (DSLR) or open the shutter (mirrorless — use Sensor Cleaning mode in the menu)
4. Hold the camera with the sensor facing down (gravity helps)
5. Insert the blower tip into the camera body (don't touch the sensor!)
6. Give 5-10 firm squeezes
7. Reattach the lens and test

Warning: Never touch the sensor with the blower tip. If you do, stop and proceed to Method 2.

Method 2: Sensor Swabs (For Stubborn Spots)

Best for: Oil spots, sticky residue, dust that won't blow off

What you need:
- Sensor swabs in the correct size (APS-C or full-frame)
- Sensor cleaning solution (methanol-based, like Eclipse)
- A clean, dust-free environment

Steps:
1. Power off your camera
2. Remove the lens and lock the sensor in cleaning mode
3. Hold the camera with the sensor facing down
4. Apply 2-3 drops of cleaning solution to the swab tip
5. Place the swab at one edge of the sensor
6. Gently drag the swab across the sensor in one smooth motion
7. Don't stop mid-way or reverse direction
8. Discard the swab (never reuse)
9. Test the result — repeat with a fresh swab if needed

Pro tip: Use one pass per swab. If you need another pass, use a fresh swab. Reusing a swab just redistributes the dirt.

Method 3: Sensor Cleaning Kits

Best for: Regular maintenance

Recommended kits:
- VSGO Sensor Cleaning Kit ($30): Includes swabs, solution, and a blower. Great value.
- VisibleDust Arctic Butterfly ($60): A spinning brush that charges statically to attract dust. Excellent for quick cleanings.
- Photographic Solutions SensorKlear ($25): A pen with a sticky tip. Good for spot-cleaning individual dust specks.

Prevention

Keep your sensor clean longer:
- Change lenses quickly in sheltered areas
- Point the camera down when changing lenses
- Use a lens cap on both the camera and lens
- Clean your rear lens elements regularly
- Use a UV filter on lenses (keeps dust off the front element)
- Store cameras with a body cap, not a lens
- Use a camera bag with minimal lint

When to Send It to a Professional

Send to a pro if:
- You've tried swabs and the spots remain
- There's visible damage or scratches on the sensor
- You're not confident in your ability to do it safely
- Your camera has a sticky shutter or other mechanical issues

Professional cleaning cost: $50-100, often free with a camera service. Many camera stores offer same-day sensor cleaning.

Our Advice

Start with the blower — it solves 80% of dust problems. If you need swabs, buy a quality kit and follow the instructions exactly. One bad cleaning can scratch the sensor coating, turning a $20 problem into a $500 repair. When in doubt, pay a professional.
