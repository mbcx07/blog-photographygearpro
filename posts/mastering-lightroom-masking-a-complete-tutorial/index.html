Mastering Lightroom Masking: A Complete Tutorial

2026-07-29 · Editing · Tags: Lightroom, masking, photo editing tutorial, local adjustments
## Lightroom Masking: The Complete Guide Adobe's AI-powered masking tools, introduced in 2021 and refined ever since, have transformed how photographers edit. What once required Photoshop and hours of work can now be done in seconds with incredible accuracy. ### The Masking Panel Access masking by clicking the circle icon (or pressing M) in Lightroom Classic. The panel offers several mask types: **1. Subject Mask** Lightroom's AI identifies the main subject of your photo with remarkable accuracy. One click creates a mask around people, animals, or objects. **Use cases:** - Brightening a backlit portrait subject - Adding contrast to a bird against a bright sky - Applying sharpening only to the subject - Reducing exposure on a product against a white background **Pro tip:** After creating a subject mask, click "Subtract" and use "Sky" to remove the sky from the mask if it was incorrectly included. **2. Sky Mask** Instantly selects the sky, even through complex tree branches or buildings. This is the tool that made graduated filters almost obsolete for many landscape photographers. **Use cases:** - Darkening an overexposed sky - Adding clarity and dehaze to clouds - Applying a warm tone to sunset skies - Replacing the sky (requires Photoshop, but the mask exports perfectly) **3. Background Mask** The inverse of subject mask — selects everything that isn't the main subject. Perfect for applying a subtle blur or darkening the background to make your subject pop. **4. People Mask** Advanced AI that identifies individual people and their body parts. You can select specific people in a group photo and even target eyes, lips, skin, teeth, or clothing individually. **Use cases:** - Whitening teeth without affecting skin tone - Brightening eyes in portraits - Smoothing skin while keeping texture - Adjusting exposure on each person in a group shot **5. Brush and Linear/Radial Gradients** The classic tools are still here and more powerful than ever. The brush now has auto-mask that detects edges as you paint, making precise selections much easier. ### Advanced Masking Techniques **Intersect Masks** Combine two masks to create a precise selection. For example: - Create a subject mask - Create a linear gradient from the top - Intersect them to only affect the top portion of your subject **Range Masks** Add luminance, color, or depth range to any mask: - **Luminance range:** Only affect shadows, midtones, or highlights within your mask - **Color range:** Only affect specific colors within your mask - **Depth range:** Only affect objects at certain distances (requires depth-capable cameras) ### Workflow Example: Landscape Edit 1. Apply a Sky mask, reduce exposure -0.7, add +20 clarity 2. Create a Subject mask for the foreground tree, add +0.5 exposure 3. Add a Linear Gradient from the bottom, increase exposure +0.3 4. Intersect the gradient with a Luminance Range (shadows only) to avoid blowing out highlights 5. Create a Color Range mask for the autumn leaves, increase saturation +15 ### Keyboard Shortcuts | Action | Shortcut | |--------|----------| | Open masking panel | M | | Add mask | Ctrl+M (Cmd+M) | | Invert mask | Ctrl+I (Cmd+I) | | Show/hide mask overlay | O | | Cycle overlay colors | Shift+O | | Brush size | [ and ] | | Brush feather | Shift+[ and Shift+] | | Brush flow | 0-9 (percentage) | ### Our Advice Master the Subject and Sky masks first — they'll cover 80% of your editing needs. Then explore People masks for portrait work and Range masks for fine-tuning. The key is to practice on real photos and experiment with combinations.
