Presets vs Manual Editing: Finding Your Workflow

The debate between preset users and manual editors is heated. Lens en Amazon. Presets promise speed and consistency; manual editing offers control and creativity. The truth is, most professionals use both.

The Case for Presets

Speed: A good preset can transform a photo in one click. For event photographers delivering 1,000+ images, presets are essential for meeting deadlines.

Consistency: Presets ensure every image in a series has the same look. Wedding photographers, in particular, rely on presets for consistent color grading across an entire gallery.

Inspiration: Sometimes a preset gives you a look you wouldn't have thought of. It can be a creative starting point that you then refine.

Learning tool: Analyzing what a preset does teaches you about color grading, tone curves, and split toning.

Best for: Weddings, events, batch editing, social media content, consistent brand looks

The Case for Manual Editing

Control: Every image is unique. A preset that works perfectly for one photo may look terrible on another. Manual editing lets you respond to each image's specific needs.

Creativity: Presets can make your work look like everyone else's. Manual editing develops your personal style and artistic voice.

Quality: Presets can't read your image. They apply the same adjustments regardless of exposure, color balance, or subject matter. Manual editing produces higher-quality results.

Understanding: Manual editing teaches you to see light, color, and tone. You develop an intuitive understanding of what makes an image work.

Best for: Fine art, portfolio work, commercial projects, creative personal work

The Hybrid Approach (Recommended)

Most professionals use a hybrid workflow:

  1. Start with a base preset that gets you 70% of the way there
  2. Adjust exposure and white balance for the specific image
  3. Use masking to fine-tune different areas
  4. Apply genre-specific adjustments (skin smoothing for portraits, dehaze for landscapes)
  5. Export with consistent settings

Creating Your Own Presets

The best presets are the ones you create for yourself. Here's how:

Step 1: Edit a photo from start to finish manually
Step 2: Save the settings as a user preset
Step 3: Apply it to similar photos and note what needs adjustment
Step 4: Refine the preset based on common adjustments
Step 5: Create variations (warm, cool, high-contrast, soft)

Pro tip: Create presets for specific scenarios, not generic looks. A "Golden Hour Portrait" preset, a "Cloudy Landscape" preset, and a "Studio Product" preset will be more useful than a single "Beautiful" preset.

When to Buy Presets

Buy presets when:
- You're new to editing and want to learn
- You need a specific look (film emulation, vintage, etc.)
- You want to save time on batch edits
- You admire a particular photographer's style

Don't buy presets when:
- You expect them to work perfectly on every photo
- You want to develop your own style
- You're not willing to adjust them for each image

Our Verdict

Use presets for efficiency, manual editing for quality. Start with a preset that matches your vision, then spend time refining it for each image. The photographers who produce the best work don't choose one or the other — they use both strategically.

The golden rule: Never deliver a photo that only had a preset applied. Every image deserves at least basic adjustments for exposure, white balance, and composition.
